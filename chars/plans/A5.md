# Package A5: Visuals, rendering and performance

## Package-specific instructions
- Terrain relief from package A4 exists now: keep it working with every change (shadows, fog, ground shading).
- Priorities: performance first (culling, LOD, instancing or merged static batches, partial scene updates when trees fall or buildings break, cheaper fragment shading tiers, adaptive resolution / dynamic DPR to hold ~60 fps, memory), then whole-map shadows with good bias/PCF, HDR-ish bloom and color grading, weather per map (snow at Bastogne, rain/mist in the Ardennes, dust in El Alamein), night lighting (headlights as spot lights, flares, burning wrecks lights that last as long as flames), animated destruction (trees falling, houses collapsing over time), particles quality (soft particles, fog-consistent smoke, fewer allocations), height fog/sun glow, clouds/stars/time of day variety per wave, foliage reacting to blasts and tanks, rotational camera shake already done by B3 (check), obscuration (crit10: muzzle-blast dust, wreck smoke and dust clouds block vision for the AI and the player, consistent with the AI LOS code).
- Metal simulation cost (crit11): move it off the critical path (budgeted steps per frame, or skip when not visible).
- Mobile preset from package B4 exists: make the quality tiers meaningfully different.
- Measure frame times before/after (playwright 3D runs; swiftshader is CPU-bound so relative comparisons only) and report numbers.

## Audit findings to implement (verify each against the current code first: line numbers may have shifted since the audit; earlier packages may have changed things)

### [visu00] Static scenery is drawn in full twice per frame with no culling or LOD: 1.93M vertices per pass in the Ardennes, 86% of them bush leaves
- kind/impact/effort: performance/5/L
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js, chars/gl3d.js
- where: world3d.js buildBushes (l.635-698), buildScene (l.457-630), render() l.373 (shadow pass draws every item) and l.383 (main pass); game.js render3D items l.2702, setup3D l.2472-2482
- problem: Measured with a drawArrays hook on #game3d at ?level=2: 1,929,090 vertices into the shadow FBO plus 1,931,034 on screen every frame. Static meshes per level: bush3 is 1,662,972 vertices in the Ardennes (554K leaf triangles, 66.5 MB of 40-byte vertices), 730K on Kursk and 863K at Bastogne; scene3 is 493K on Kursk (185 deciduous trees of 11 spheres each) and 239K in Stalingrad. Each is one monolithic VBO, drawn whole in both passes whatever the camera can see. There is no frustum culling, no chunking and no distance LOD: every bush keeps all 120 to 313 leaves per plant at 200 m. The quality option (game.js l.4021-4022) only changes DPR and the particle cap, never the geometry. On tile-based mobile GPUs (Mali, Adreno, PowerVR), 1.3M triangles per pass overflows the parameter buffer and costs far more than its share.
- proposal: 1) Chunking. In buildScene and buildBushes, assign each triangle to a 32 m cell by its centroid, giving one Builder per cell. Return [{mesh, aabb:[min,max]}] and keep the ground quads as their own mesh. In W3D.render, cull per item. Main pass: test the AABB against 6 frustum planes taken from cam.vp (Gribb-Hartmann). Shadow pass: transform the 8 AABB corners by lvp and reject if they are all outside [-1,1] on one axis. 2) Bush LOD per chunk, built at the same time. LOD0 = everything. LOD1 = core sphere plus the 'cluster' leaves only (k >= nl in plant(), about 31% of the leaves, scaled 1.3x). LOD2 = core sphere only (6 segments). Pick by eye-to-chunk distance: <45 m LOD0, <110 m LOD1, beyond that LOD2. The shadow pass always uses LOD1 or LOD2. 3) Leaf density per quality tier: 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.35 (multiply nl). 4) Later step: 8 plant archetypes drawn with ANGLE_instanced_arrays (exposed here), with per-instance position, scale, yaw and tint. Target: under 400K vertices per pass in the Ardennes. Check with /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Game-/ac915cfe-786c-573a-8bb1-e31901f2c033/scratchpad/visaudit/perf.js (vertsPerFrame per pass), plus before/after screenshots at the same seed.

### [visu02] Shadows cut off with a hard edge about 90 m ahead (±74 m to the sides); bake a whole-map static shadow map once and keep a small per-frame map for tanks only
- kind/impact/effort: realism/5/M
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js
- where: world3d.js render() l.361-374 (ortho centred on cam.target, ext from the caller), shadowAt() l.49-58 (`return 1.0` outside the box); game.js render3D l.2808 shadowExt = sight.on ? 110 : max(60, cam3.dist*1.6)
- problem: The only shadow map is a 2048² ortho box with half-extent 73.6 m in chase view, centred on cam3.target (8 to 20 m ahead of the tank). In gunner sight it is 110 m, centred 60 m ahead. Anything outside is fully lit (shadowAt returns 1.0) and the transition is a hard square edge. The Kursk screenshot shows it: foreground trees cast shadows, while the houses and trees further back, and the far Panzer IV, have none. Rendering that box also costs a full pass over every static vertex (1.93M in the Ardennes) every frame, even though the sun never moves within a level.
- proposal: Static map, rendered once in setup3D and after each rebuild. Build the light view from the map centre and fit the ortho bounds tightly to the 8 corners of the map AABB (0..WW/PX, -WH/PX..0, z 0..18 m) in light space. The scene is 371x243 m, so 4096² gives about 10 cm texels; use 2048² on mobile. Use the WEBGL_depth_texture extension (exposed here) for a DEPTH_COMPONENT texture with no RGBA packing pass. Draw scene3 and the LOD1 bushes into it. Dynamic map: 1024² or 2048² centred on cam.target with ext 60–110 m, containing only tanks, turrets, the commander and debris (about 20K vertices per tank). In the FS: `sh = min(shadowAt(uShadowS, vLS, bS), shadowAt(uShadow, vL, bD))`, with vLS = uLVPS * w computed in the VS. When one chunk is rebuilt, redraw only that chunk's light-space rectangle using gl.scissor, without clearing the rest. Wind sway (3–4 cm) is missing from the static shadows; that is acceptable. Result: shadows everywhere out to the fog, and the per-frame shadow pass drops from 1.93M to about 0.2M vertices. Check by repeating the screenshot l1 crop (distant houses should be shadowed) and by vertsPerFrame on the FBO in perf.js.

### [visu03] Shadow bias equals 0.55–1.47 m, so shadows peter-pan and running gear never self-shadows; shadow VS sway does not match the main VS; no texel snapping; blocky NEAREST 3x3 PCF; no contact AO under tanks
- kind/impact/effort: realism/4/S
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js
- where: world3d.js FS l.181 bias `0.0012 + 0.002*(1-abs(nd))`, ortho near/far l.365 (20..480), SVS l.205-206 vs VS l.15-28, shadowAt l.49-58 NEAREST (l.272), FS l.183-184 ao (ground forced to 1)
- problem: The ortho depth range is 460 m, so the normalised bias works out to 0.55 m for surfaces facing the sun and 1.47 m at grazing angles. On flat ground at Kursk (sun elevation 36°) the bias is 0.93 m along the ray. Occluders less than about 0.55 m above a receiver therefore never shadow it, and every shadow starts about 0.75 m away from its caster. Fenders never shade the road wheels, the turret overhang never shades the hull sides, and tanks look like they hover (the ground also gets ao = 1, so there is no contact darkening). The shadow VS only applies the x part of the foliage sway, while the main VS applies x and y plus the per-leaf flutter. Caster and receiver disagree by up to 0.025·h metres. Today the huge bias hides this; fixing the bias would turn it into crawling acne on canopies. The light box follows the continuous cam.target, and ext changes with mouse-wheel zoom, so shadow edges crawl as you drive or zoom. The 9 NEAREST taps give a stair-stepped penumbra with 10 levels.
- proposal: Use normal-offset shadows. In the VS: `vec3 nW = normalize(mat3(uModel)*aNor); vL = uLVP * vec4(w.xyz + nW*uNOff, 1.0);` with uNOff = 1.5 × (2·ext/SM), about 0.1 m. Drop the FS bias to 0.00025 + 0.0005·(1−|nd|). Tighten the ortho to near 140 / far 300 (eyeL stays at 220 m), which is about 3x more depth precision. Put the displacement in one GLSL string, `vec4 sway(vec4 w, float mat, vec3 aPos)`, and concatenate it into both VS and SVS. Snap: transform c into light view, round x and y to the texel size 2·ext/SM, transform back, and quantise ext to 10 m steps. PCF: 4x4 bilinear-weighted taps using fract(uv*SM), or 8 Poisson taps rotated by h3(gl_FragCoord.xyx). Under WebGL2, use sampler2DShadow with LINEAR for hardware 2x2 PCF per tap. Contact AO: pass uTk[10] (x, y, cos, sin) and uTkH[10] (half length, half width). In the ground branch: `vec2 q = mat2(c,-s,s,c)*(vW.xy-p); float d = length(max(abs(q)-hs,0.0)); ao *= 1.0 - 0.45*(1.0-smoothstep(0.0,1.3,d));`. Check with close-camera crops of a Tiger's running gear before and after, and by watching Ardennes canopies for acne after the change.

### [visu04] Every tree knocked down or wall or house damage step rebuilds and re-uploads the ENTIRE static scene (176 ms of JS on Kursk)
- kind/impact/effort: performance/4/M
- files: chars/game.js, chars/world3d.js
- where: game.js rebuildScene3 l.2483-2487, throttle l.2693 (3x per second), triggers knockTree l.2083, damageObstacle l.2062/2065, tanks ramming trees l.918; world3d.js buildScene l.457-630 (rebuilds the 3200 grass tufts and 140 out-of-map trees every time)
- problem: scene3Dirty is set whenever a tree falls (including tanks driving through them), when a building or wall crosses one sixth of its HP, and when an obstacle dies. rebuildScene3 then re-runs buildScene for every obstacle, all the grass and the 140 out-of-map trees, copies a JS array into a Float32Array and re-uploads it. Measured in headless Chromium: buildScene alone takes 176 ms on Kursk (493,260 vertices, 19.7 MB re-uploaded), and the whole rebuild takes 228–238 ms. Stalingrad takes 76 ms of JS, the Ardennes 73 ms total. In forest or city fighting this can stutter up to 3 times per second, and a mid-range phone would be 2 to 4 times slower.
- proposal: Build the grass and out-of-map trees once, into a separate never-rebuilt mesh (or chunked as in the first finding). Store obstacles per 32 m chunk: obstacle → chunk index; damageObstacle and knockTree mark only their chunk dirty; rebuildScene3(chunk) rebuilds 5–30K vertices, which should take under 5 ms. Rebuild only chunks visible or near the camera first, spread over frames (at most 1 per frame). Builder: write into a pre-sized or doubling Float32Array instead of this.v.push (gl3d.js l.67/72). Then use bufferSubData if the size is unchanged, or a fresh buffer per chunk. Check with the rebuildMs metric in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Game-/ac915cfe-786c-573a-8bb1-e31901f2c033/scratchpad/visaudit/info2.js, and with the p95 frame time over 20 s of ?auto&level=2.

### [visu06] Fragment shading is heavy and the quality tiers barely change it: about 170 hash evaluations per ground pixel, DPR 2 with MSAA as the default for everyone, no dynamic resolution
- kind/impact/effort: performance/4/M
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js, chars/meta.js
- where: world3d.js FS ground l.138-142 (fbm x4 + n3), l.168 grime fbm computed for every material, light loop l.188-194, shadowAt l.49-58; context l.249 antialias:true; game.js QDPR/PCAP l.197, resize l.199-206, applyOptions l.4018-4024; meta.js l.141 default quality 'haute'
- problem: Per ground pixel: g1 and g2 fbm (8 n3), g3 (1 n3), the bump from two more fbm (8 n3) and grime fbm (4 n3, computed for every material even though only paint, steel and burnt use it). That is 21 value-noise evaluations of 8 hashes each (about 170 hashes), plus 9 unpacked shadow taps and an 8-iteration light loop that always runs fully even with no lights. 'haute' is the default profile setting, so every device gets DPR up to 2 (a 3x phone renders at 2x) plus MSAA. The quality setting only changes DPR and PCAP. Shadow size, PCF taps, foliage density and shader cost never scale, and nothing adapts to the measured frame time.
- proposal: 1) Bake the noise into a 256x256 RGBA8 tiling texture with mipmaps, generated at startup from the existing fbm: R and G = fbm at two scales, B and A = detail normal xy. In the ground branch, 3 texture2D lookups at different scales replace the 17 n3 calls; mipmapping also removes the aliasing. 2) `float grime = (vM < 3.5 || uBurnt > 0.0) ? fbm(P*5.0) : 0.5;`. 3) Add `uniform float uNL;` and `if (float(i) >= uNL) break;` in the light loop. 4) Prepend #defines when compiling: QUALITY 0/1/2 → SHADOW_TAPS 1/4/9, MAX_LIGHTS 2/4/8, and SM 1024/2048. With antialias chosen at context creation (low = false). 5) Default quality from the device: matchMedia('(pointer: coarse)') or a mobile UA → 'moyenne' with DPR cap 1.25; MSAA off on low. 6) Dynamic resolution: keep an EMA of frame time. Above 20 ms for 1 s, scale −= 0.1 (min 0.6); below 13 ms for 3 s, scale += 0.05. Call W3D.render(..., round(W*DPR*scale), round(H*DPR*scale)) and let the CSS stretch it (the canvas is already 100vw/100vh). 7) Add an FPS and render-scale readout on ?debug. Check with the perf2.js variant method (/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Game-/ac915cfe-786c-573a-8bb1-e31901f2c033/scratchpad/visaudit/perf2.js, 'cheapfs' variant) on an unloaded machine, and on a real Android device through Chrome remote devtools.

### [visu07] The ground texture aliases (NPOT without mipmaps or anisotropy, unfiltered procedural noise) and is stretched into streaks beyond the map edges
- kind/impact/effort: bug/4/S
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js
- where: world3d.js setGround l.298-304 (LINEAR, no mipmaps), FS l.136-142 (uv clamp, g3 = n3(vW.xy*9.0), finite-difference bump); game.js buildGround l.383-437 (26,000 2x2-px speckles, roads drawn to the edges), WW/WH l.185; world3d.js l.467-468 (ground extends 0.6x the map beyond the edges), l.620 (no out-of-map tree line in desert and city)
- problem: The 2600x1700 ground canvas is a non-power-of-two texture, so WebGL1 cannot mipmap it. It is sampled with LINEAR minification only, and the anisotropic extension is never used even though EXT_texture_filter_anisotropic is exposed. At chase pitch (30°) each pixel covers 2 to 4 texels at 100 m, so the 2x2 speckles, the track stamps and the crater edges shimmer while driving. The FS detail octave g3 has a period of about 11 cm and the bump fbm has no derivative fade (Zimmerit fades with distance, l.113, the ground does not), which causes sparkle and moiré. The ground plane runs 222 m past the map but samples a clamped uv, so border texels stretch into long streaks. This is visible in the Stalingrad tactical view looking west, where the city roads reach the map edge; desert and city also have no tree line to hide it.
- proposal: Upload to a power-of-two texture (4096x2048, by drawing groundCv scaled into a POT canvas, or directly when on WebGL2). Call gl.generateMipmap after dirty-tile uploads, at most every 0.5 s. Use LINEAR_MIPMAP_LINEAR plus anisotropy 8 (4 on mobile). In the FS, enable GL_OES_standard_derivatives: `float fw = max(fwidth(vW.x), fwidth(vW.y)); g3 = mix(0.5, g3, 1.0-smoothstep(0.03,0.09,fw)); bump *= 1.0-smoothstep(0.1,0.35,fw);`. Outside the map: `float o = max(max(-uv.x, uv.x-1.0), max(-uv.y, uv.y-1.0)); base = mix(base, uOuter*(0.85+0.3*g1), smoothstep(0.0,0.02,o));`, with uOuter = lv.ground[0]. For desert and city, add an edge dressing (dune ridges, ruined facades as cheap boxes). Check with a 10-frame screenshot sequence while panning in tactical view, comparing the shimmer, and a view toward the west edge in Stalingrad.

### [visu08] Memory spikes when a level loads (about 200 MB transient in the Ardennes), 40-byte non-indexed vertices, tank meshes duplicated across two contexts
- kind/impact/effort: performance/4/M
- files: chars/gl3d.js, chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js
- where: gl3d.js Builder l.54-72 (this.v.push of 10 doubles per vertex), upload l.322-327; world3d.js upload/bindMesh l.281-294; game.js MESH3 l.2443-2445, R3 l.246, dark canvas l.195-204
- problem: Builder stores vertices in plain JS number arrays. The Ardennes bushes alone are 16.6M numbers (about 133 MB of heap), then copied into a 66.5 MB Float32Array and uploaded as a 66.5 MB VBO. Measured buildBushes takes 0.75–1.0 s in headless Chromium. Each vertex carries a float normal, a float colour and a float material id (40 B) with no index buffer, so spheres and cylinders repeat every vertex about 6 times. Each tank model is uploaded to both W3D and R3. The 2D 'dark' canvas is allocated at full DPR even in 3D mode, where it is never used. Older iOS Safari tabs get killed at about 300–400 MB, so the forest level could crash on mobile.

Update bindMesh to use vertexAttribPointer with those types (the shaders stay unchanged; aMat arrives as a float). Builder writes into a growable ArrayBuffer and DataView. Use indexed spheres and cylinders (OES_element_index_uint is available). Instance the plants as in the first finding. Do not allocate 'dark' when W3D exists, and free the scene VBOs on toMenu. Expected result: Ardennes VBOs from about 85 MB to under 15 MB, and level start about 70% faster. Check with performance.memory.usedJSHeapSize sampled during startLevel in ?debug, plus the buildBushMs metric in info.js.

### [visu09] No HDR post-processing: glow particles clip in LDR, no bloom, no colour grading, the vignette is drawn on the 2D canvas
- kind/impact/effort: feature/4/M
- files: chars/world3d.js, chars/game.js
- where: world3d.js tm() l.60 applied in the FS l.73/197, PFS glow branch l.235-238 (alpha up to 3, additive), create() l.249; game.js vignette l.2815-2818
- problem: Tone mapping runs inside every shader, so additive muzzle flashes, fires and tracers (intensity 1.6–3) clip to flat white with no halo. Explosions, burning wrecks at night and tracer streaks lack the glow that sells them. There is no per-level mood grading: the steppe, forest, ruins, desert and night all go through the same ACES curve. The vignette is a CPU radial gradient on the HUD canvas.
- proposal: Add a WebGL2 path (getContext('webgl2'), falling back to the current WebGL1 code). Render target: a multisampled RGBA16F renderbuffer (with EXT_color_buffer_float or half-float; samples = min(4, MAX_SAMPLES)) plus DEPTH24. Shaders output linear HDR: remove tm() and keep the fog. blitFramebuffer into an RGBA16F texture. Bloom: a half-resolution prefilter `max(c - 1.0, 0)` with soft knee 0.5, then 5 downsamples (13-tap, Jimenez 2014) and tent upsamples. Composite on a fullscreen triangle: `c = hdr + bloom*0.05; c *= uExposure; c = ACES(c); c = pow(max(c*uGain + uLift, 0.0), 1.0/uGamma); c = mix(vec3(dot(c, vec3(0.299,0.587,0.114))), c, uSat); c *= 1.0 - 0.38*smoothstep(0.35, 0.9, length(uv-0.5)*1.4); c += (h(uv+t)-0.5)*0.012;`. Per-level grades in ENV: Kursk warm (gain 1.03/1.0/0.94, saturation 1.05), Ardennes cool green (saturation 0.9), Stalingrad desaturated (saturation 0.7, lift +0.02), El Alamein bright and warm, Bastogne blue night (saturation 0.6, gain 0.9/0.95/1.1). Add mild chromatic aberration in sight view and a red edge pulse when the player is hit. Estimated cost: about 1–1.5 ms at 1080p on an integrated GPU; disable bloom on 'basse'. Check with before/after screenshots of fx.js (explosion at 0.3 s) and a night wreck.

### [visu12] Destruction appears instantly: trees switch to fallen trunks, houses switch to rubble, building damage is black boxes

Check with ?debug: fire HE at a house and a tree, and capture frames at 0.3, 1 and 3 s.
